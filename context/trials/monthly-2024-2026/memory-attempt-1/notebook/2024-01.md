Rule 1: Entry trigger  
Enter only if symbol gains ≥1% over prior 3 daily bars AND close exceeds prior 5-day high.  
Evidence [P1]: MSFT rallied +6.34% for the period but agent entered Jan 11 after 4 days of cash holding following mild declines Jan 2-8, missing the early run. AAPL -1.47% period return with no trigger fired. Unproven: whether this filter produces earlier entries without whipsaws across multiple periods.

Rule 2: Position sizing  
Allocate 20% of equity per entry, reserving 5% for scaling.  
Evidence [P1]: AAPL and MSFT each hit ~24-25% of equity, leaving zero capacity to add to MSFT as it rose +6.34%. With 20% sizing, agent could have deployed ~5% extra into MSFT at lower prices, improving return. Supported: 20% permits one scaling tranche while respecting 25% max. Unproven: long-term edge of 20% vs 25% sizing.

Rule 3: Scaling rule  
If a position gains ≥3% from entry and occupies ≥15% of equity, add up to 5% additional equity in 1/4 original-size increments on pullbacks ≤2% from peak.  
Evidence [P1]: MSFT rose +6.34% but hard 25% cap prevented any scaling. AAPL declined -1.47% with no partial-exit rule. Supported: scaling into winners on pullbacks captures trend while controlling concentration. Unproven: pullback frequency and fill slippage.

Rule 4: Exit rule  
Sell 50% of a position if it declines ≥5% from entry, or after 15 calendar days without a new higher high.  
Evidence [P1]: AAPL held from Jan 9 with no sell trigger; MSFT held entire period with +3% paper gain, no partial-take-profit triggered. Mistake: holding through reversals without booking profits. Supported: clear exit limits downside and frees capital. Unproven: 5%/15-day thresholds optimality.

Rule 5: Cash deployment  
Deploy full equity within 5 trading days of a signal; if no qualifying symbol, hold cash but cap cash allocation at ≤20% of equity to force rotation into outperforming names.  
Evidence [P1]: Agent held cash Jan 2-8, then deployed, but missed early MSFT/SPY gains (+6.34%, +2.27%). By Jan 9/11 positions were size-limited, preventing further participation. Idle cash in a rising market eroded relative performance (+0.752% agent vs +0% B&H). Supported: limiting cash allocation prevents prolonged idleness. Unproven: 20% cash cap effect on signal capture rate.
